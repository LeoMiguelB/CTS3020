# Post 2: Demystifying Dark Money Through Audio

Planet Money, a podcast produced by NPR, published their 390th episode on July 27, 2012 ("Episode 390"). In this specific episode, they talk about the inner workings of shell companies by going through the process of setting up one. By going through the process, from calling the intermediaries to registering the company, they demystify the complexity around shell companies; as it turns out, it is not all that hard. By using audio affordances such as intimacy and the pacing of transmedia audio storytelling, Planet Money is effectively able to convey how easy it is to set up shell companies that those in power, such as corrupt politicians, use to evade taxation.

In the podcast, they begin the operation of setting up a shell company by calling an intermediary on the phone and saying, "Hi, my name is Chana Joffe-Walt. I'm calling hoping to set up a shell company somewhere offshore..." ("Episode 390"). They then follow a series of steps where they eventually successfully register a shell company. During some scenes, they share laughs with the company they are calling, which helps the audience feel more connected. It is through this audio intimacy that they are effectively able to convey the ideas around these mechanisms that are usually buried in big walls of text. Additionally, during this part of the podcast, the narrative they portray is heist-like - paced fast, and the mood is very upbeat - which effectively uses the concept of narrative pacing to expose these loopholes that many corrupt politicians use.

Moreover, in Amanda Hill's framework she mentions the concept of ethical storytelling (Hill). Essentially it's about telling stories that challenge the power dynamics that allow the elites' narratives to dominate. There is a lot of complexity in the finance world, and this is where politicians use it to their advantage because they know the general public won't have the necessary resources that would allow them to understand it. Episode 390 of Planet Money completely challenges this by showcasing the steps of obtaining a shell company live. This specific format of ethical storytelling gives the general audience the power to understand this side of the finance world that would have otherwise been hidden from them.

In Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie's "The Danger of a Single Story", she mentions how a single story is often used for control (Adichie). This concept of a single story applies to the idea of how the financial world has often been only understood by those who are wealthy, including politicians. With this podcast format, Planet Money is able to combat that single story which the wealthy have been trying to maintain and present a simple, intuitive presentation for the general public to consume.

Ultimately, Planet Money demonstrates the effectiveness of using audio as the medium through which complex systems can be explained. Through the use of intimacy in audio and transmedia audio pacing, they were effectively able to tell a story that gave the general public a sense of the system that the wealthy use.
